How to review app permissions on Android

Apps may request access to parts of your phone, such as the camera, microphone, contacts, or location. Here is how to review those choices and make informed decisions.

When you install or open an app, it may ask for permission to use a feature or information on your device. Some requests make sense for the app's purpose. A map app, for example, may need location access while navigation is running. Other requests deserve a closer look.

Before you start: Android menus vary by phone maker and software version. The wording below is a general path; use Settings search if a menu has a different name on your device.

1. Review permissions for one app

  1. Open your phone's Settings.
  2. Tap Apps (on some phones this may be called Apps & notifications or App management).
  3. Choose the app you want to review. You may need to tap See all apps first.
  4. Open Permissions to see which categories the app can access and which are not allowed.
  5. Tap a permission to review the available choices. Depending on the permission, you may see options such as Allow only while using the app, Ask every time, or Don't allow.

2. Pay attention to sensitive permissions

Take a moment to review access to these areas:

  • Location: Does the app need your location for the feature you use?
  • Camera and microphone: Are these needed for taking photos, video calls, scanning, or voice features?
  • Contacts: Does the app genuinely need to read or manage your contacts?
  • Photos and videos: Can you grant access only to selected photos where your Android version offers that choice?
  • Notifications: Are the alerts useful, or would you rather turn them off?

3. Check the privacy dashboard

On supported Android versions, the Privacy dashboard can show which permissions apps have used recently. Open Settings and search for Privacy dashboard or Permission manager. Review the categories that matter to you and investigate access you do not recognize.

4. Change access thoughtfully

If a permission does not fit the app's purpose, consider denying it or choosing a more limited option. The app may stop offering a feature that depends on that permission. You can usually revisit the setting later if you change your mind.

Do not deny everything automatically. Some permissions are necessary for an app's core function. Check what the app does and what feature you are trying to use before changing access.

5. Make it a regular habit

Review permissions when you install an unfamiliar app, after a major app update, or whenever something seems unusual. Remove apps you no longer use, and install apps from sources you trust.

The takeaway

You do not need to inspect every setting every day. Start with apps that access your location, camera, microphone, contacts, or photos. Keep permissions that support features you actually use, and limit access that does not make sense for you.
